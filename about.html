I’m a product manager with a web development background.

I like working through what a product needs to do, what it will take to build, and whether it’s worth the investment.

Before moving into development and product management, I spent seven years in corporate communications and investor relations at The Washington Post Company. I prepared presentations for the chairman, worked on the annual report, and learned how a publicly traded company explains its results to shareholders.

When the newspaper was sold and my role ended, a colleague suggested I learn HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. I took the advice. My next job was as a front-end developer working on PBGC.gov, a federal website people turn to for information about their pensions.

From there, I joined Green America as digital product manager and web developer. For nearly seven years, I was the only product manager and the only developer on staff. I managed a website serving roughly 935,000 annual users and supporting more than $650,000 in tracked online revenue. I also led planning for its redesign and took Get a Better Bank, a standalone product, from concept to launch.

The work ranged from making the case to leadership for a vendor whose quote was three times our budget—but whose proposed build would cost less to run—to writing a Python script that kept a 30,000-point map updated after the vendor’s upload tool hit its limit. I’m comfortable making those decisions and doing the hands-on work behind them.

I use AI tools daily. At Green America, I used them to bring development and payment-compliance work in-house, reducing our spending on outside developers. I reviewed and tested all of that work before it went live.

I’ve also taught English in Kumamoto, Japan, and I’m an actor and acting teacher.

Board service

Green America — employee-elected board member

My colleagues elected me twice to represent employees on the board, where I served on the finance committee. That gave me a different view of the organization’s budget and strategy, and it still shapes how I weigh product decisions.

Studio Acting Conservatory — board member and acting teacher

I’ve taught voice and acting at the conservatory since 2018. I also serve on the board, where I advised on a $60,000 website rebuild.

Teaching and performing have made me more attentive to how people communicate—what they say, where they hesitate, and whether an explanation is actually landing.

New Endeavors by Women — former board member

I served for four years on the board of this DC women’s shelter.

What I’m looking for next

I’m looking for a full-time product management role where I can take responsibility for how a product performs—not just what ships. I want to work closely with engineering and customers, with ownership of adoption, retention, and revenue as well as delivery.

I’m particularly interested in technology, financial services, legal, and media companies. Clear expectations, room to grow, and a product with a clear business purpose matter to me.
